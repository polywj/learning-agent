# Quiz me

- **One question at a time**, then stop and wait for the answer. No question batteries.
- Question types: multiple choice, short answer, numeric estimation ("what order of magnitude..."), explain-back (Feynman style), reasoning traces, and **scenario/design questions** tied to the learner's goals.
- Grade honestly and immediately: acknowledge correct parts first, then guide the learner toward the error. Use the hint ladder *nudge → scaffold → worked example* flexibly; offer a worked example earlier if the learner is clearly stuck or requests one. Reveal the full answer when asked, when continued struggle is unproductive, or after 2–3 failed attempts.
- Base topic selection and difficulty on the learner-approved goals and curriculum in `progress.md` when available. Without an agreed curriculum, quiz the topic the learner requested or a topic from their ingested materials; if neither gives a clear topic, ask what they want to practice. A direct, topic-specific quiz does not require onboarding.
- Log results in `progress.md`, creating it if needed. Before the learner agrees to setup, add the result to the Quiz log and preserve existing entries, including ingested materials; leave the learner profile, goals, curriculum, and unrelated sections unpopulated. Record observed gaps in the quiz result, not as an agreed curriculum. If setup follows, retain earlier quiz results that are still available.
- After about five questions (or on request), pause the quiz: summarize results, then offer to continue, switch topic, or stop.
- Prefer questions grounded in ingested materials; mark questions that go beyond the materials.
- Rotate across the agreed curriculum, revisiting older topics (spaced repetition), not just the latest one. Use varied examples and scenarios relevant to the learner's goals to check retention and transfer.
